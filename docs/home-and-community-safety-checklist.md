# Home and Community Safety Checklist

Practical, low-cost ways to reduce your risk. No single measure is perfect. Layers of protection work best.

## Your home

- [ ] Working locks on all doors and windows, including security gates on exterior doors
- [ ] Good outdoor lighting at entrances, ideally motion-activated
- [ ] Walls, hedges and fences kept in good repair, with trees and shrubs trimmed so intruders cannot hide
- [ ] An alarm system with an armed-response contract, if you can afford one
- [ ] Panic button or emergency contacts saved in your phone
- [ ] Spare keys never hidden outside, and never left with strangers
- [ ] Valuables, serial numbers and IMEI numbers recorded and stored safely (photos in a secure place)

## When you are out

- [ ] Stay aware of your surroundings, and avoid walking while distracted by your phone
- [ ] Keep valuables out of sight in your car and lock doors while driving
- [ ] Be careful at gates, driveways and ATMs, which are common targets
- [ ] Share your trip details with someone you trust when travelling alone

## Online and phone safety

- [ ] Never share your PIN, OTP or banking password with anyone, including people claiming to be from your bank
- [ ] Be sceptical of unexpected messages about prizes, parcels or "urgent" payments
- [ ] Use two-factor authentication on banking, email and social media
- [ ] Report fraud to your bank immediately, then open a case

## Community

- [ ] Join your local **Community Police Forum (CPF)**. Find it through your nearest police station.
- [ ] Know your neighbours and exchange emergency contact numbers
- [ ] Join or start a neighbourhood watch **registered with SAPS** and run within the law
- [ ] Report suspicious activity to the police or Crime Stop. Do not confront people yourself.
- [ ] Never share suspects' photos, names or addresses online. Let the police handle it.

## If something happens

1. Your safety comes first. Property can be replaced.
2. Call **10111** or **112** when it is safe to do so.
3. Get a case number. See [How to Report a Crime](how-to-report-a-crime.md).
4. Seek support. Victims of crime can contact local victim support services or trauma counselling organisations.
