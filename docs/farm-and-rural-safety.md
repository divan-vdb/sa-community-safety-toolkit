# Farm and Rural Community Safety Guide

Rural areas face particular challenges: long distances, slow response times and limited cellphone coverage. This guide covers practical steps for farms, smallholdings and rural villages.

> **In immediate danger?** Call **10111** or **112** from a cellphone. If you have no signal, see "Communication" below.

## Communication

- [ ] Know where your cellphone has signal on the property, and keep a charged phone, power bank or car charger available
- [ ] Save emergency numbers in every phone: 10111, 112, your nearest police station, your private security or armed-response company and your neighbours
- [ ] Set up a **radio or two-way network** with neighbours if cellphone coverage is poor
- [ ] Agree on a **code word or signal** with household members and workers for "I need help"
- [ ] Keep a written list of emergency numbers near the landline or in a common area

## Know your location

Emergency responders often struggle to find rural properties.

- [ ] Write down your **farm name, nearest town, road name, GPS coordinates** and directions from the nearest main road, and keep them where everyone can see them
- [ ] Make sure the farm gate and entrance are clearly marked and visible at night
- [ ] Share GPS coordinates with your police station, security company and neighbours
- [ ] Practise giving directions over the phone so you can do it calmly under stress

## Securing the property

- [ ] Lock gates, and keep security gates on the house in good repair
- [ ] Use outdoor lighting around the house, sheds and entrances
- [ ] Secure tools, fuel, diesel, tractors and equipment in locked sheds. Record serial numbers.
- [ ] Mark livestock, equipment and tools so they can be identified if stolen (brand marks, ear tags, engraving)
- [ ] Consider dogs, alarms and perimeter beams, which add useful layers of warning
- [ ] Keep vegetation trimmed around the homestead so people cannot approach unseen

## Livestock and stock theft

- [ ] Keep your **livestock register** up to date: numbers, brand marks, descriptions and photos
- [ ] Count your animals regularly, and note any missing immediately
- [ ] Report stock theft to the police straight away. Time matters for tracing animals.
- [ ] Do not move or disturb tracks, footprints or a theft scene before police arrive, where it is safe to leave them
- [ ] Know the rules for transporting and selling livestock, including **proof of ownership** and required documentation

## Working together

- [ ] Join your local **Community Police Forum (CPF)**. Ask your police station how.
- [ ] Join your **farm watch or rural safety network** and make sure it is **registered with SAPS** and operates within the law
- [ ] Know your neighbours and agree how you will check on each other, especially elderly or isolated residents
- [ ] Include farm workers and their families in safety plans. Everyone on the property benefits from clear communication and trust.
- [ ] Hold regular meetings to share information about suspicious activity or recurring problems

## If something happens

1. Your safety and your family's safety come first. Property can be replaced.
2. Call **10111** or **112**, then your security company and neighbours.
3. Do not pursue suspects on your own. Pursuits are dangerous and can make things worse.
4. Give police your exact location and a clear description of what happened.
5. Get a case number. See [How to Report a Crime](how-to-report-a-crime.md).
6. Seek support afterwards. Trauma affects whole families and farm communities, and counselling helps.

## For community groups

Rural safety works best when it is organised, lawful and respectful of everyone's rights. Do not share suspects' names, photos or details online, and do not take the law into your own hands. See [SAFETY.md](../SAFETY.md).
