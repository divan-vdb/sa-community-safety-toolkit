# How to Report a Crime in South Africa

> **In immediate danger?** Call **10111** (SAPS) or **112** from a cellphone. Do not wait.

## Where to report

| Situation | Who to contact |
|---|---|
| Emergency or crime in progress | **10111** or **112** |
| Report a crime in person | Any police station (you do not have to use the station closest to where it happened) |
| Anonymous tip-off | **Crime Stop: 08600 10111** or SMS **32211** |
| Gender-based violence | **GBV Command Centre: 0800 428 428** (free, 24 hours) |
| Child in danger or abused | **Childline: 116** (free) |
| Police misconduct or corruption | Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID): ipid.gov.za |

## Step by step: reporting at a police station

1. **Go to any police station** and ask to open a case. You can bring someone for support.
2. **Make a statement.** Describe what happened in your own words: what, when, where, and what you remember about any suspects. Stick to facts.
3. **Get your case number (CAS number).** Always ask for it and write it down. You need it for insurance claims, follow-ups and court.
4. **Ask for the investigating officer's name and contact details.** A case is assigned to an officer.
5. **Keep a copy** of any forms or statements you sign.

Reporting a crime is **free**. You should never have to pay to open a case.

## What to bring or note down

- Your ID (if you have it, but you can still report without it)
- Date, time and place of the incident
- A description of what was taken or damaged (serial numbers, IMEI numbers, vehicle details)
- Any witnesses, without chasing them yourself
- Photos of damage or injuries, taken safely

## After you report

- **Follow up** with the investigating officer using your case number.
- **Insurance:** most insurers need the case number within a set time, so contact them quickly.
- **If you are not being helped,** ask to speak to the station commander. You can also contact your local Community Police Forum (CPF) or IPID.

## Your rights as a victim

Under the Service Charter for Victims of Crime you have the right to be treated with respect, to be informed about your case, and to give and receive information. You also have the right to be protected from further harm.

## Please do not

- Confront suspects yourself
- Post suspects' names, photos or addresses online
- Destroy evidence or clean up a crime scene before police arrive, if it is safe to wait

## Important

Phone numbers and procedures can change. Please check the official SAPS website (saps.gov.za) for current information, and open an Issue on this repository if you find something outdated.
